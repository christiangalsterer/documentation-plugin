# Word-Level Slop

Word-level AI-slop: filler, throat-clearing, marketing, and jargon phrases,
plus adverbs and vague declarations. Replace with plain language or cut
outright. Fixes keep meaning, facts, and technical terms intact.

## Filler and Throat-Clearing Openers

Cut these announcement phrases. The sentence usually reads better without
them. State the content directly.

- "Here's the thing:"
- "Here's what [X]"
- "Here's why [X]"
- "Here's this [X]"
- "Here's that [X]"
- "It turns out"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "The truth is,"
- "Let me be clear"
- "I'm going to be honest"
- "In today's fast-paced world…"
- "It is important to note…"
- "It is worth mentioning that…"
- "In conclusion…"
- "As we all know…"

## Emphasis Crutches

These add no meaning. Delete them.

- "Full stop." / "Period."
- "Let that sink in."
- "This matters because"
- "Make no mistake"
- "Here's why that matters"

## Generic Filler

Cut or restate concretely.

- "At its core"
- "At the end of the day"
- "When it comes to"
- "In a world where"
- "The reality is"
- "It's worth noting"
- "In today's [X]"

## Adverbs

Kill all adverbs: no -ly words, no softeners, no intensifiers, no hedges. This
is a blanket rule, not a curated list — treat any adverb as suspect until the
verb carries the meaning alone.

Specific offenders:

- "really"
- "just"
- "literally"
- "genuinely"
- "honestly"
- "simply"
- "actually"
- "deeply"
- "truly"
- "fundamentally"
- "inherently"
- "inevitably"
- "interestingly"
- "importantly"
- "crucially"
- "easily", "effortlessly", "seamless(ly)", "obviously", "please"

Also rephrase these first-person/hedge constructions:

- "we", "let's" → second person ("you") or the active subject.
- "might want to" → direct imperative.

## Business and Leverage Jargon

Replace with plain language.

### Leverage verbs

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| utilize | use |
| leverage | use |
| streamline | simplify or restate concretely |
| delve | examine, look into |
| harness | concrete verb |
| unleash | concrete verb |
| empower | concrete verb |
| revolutionize | concrete verb |
| unlock | concrete verb |

### Marketing adjectives

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| robust | omit or state the specific property |
| cutting-edge | omit or state the specific property |
| best-in-class | omit or state the specific property |
| state-of-the-art | omit or state the specific property |
| best | omit |
| unparalleled | omit |
| game-changing | omit |

### Business phrases

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| Navigate (challenges) | Handle, address |
| Unpack (analysis) | Explain, examine |
| Lean into | Accept, embrace |
| Landscape (context) | Situation, field |
| Game-changer | Significant, important |

## Meta-Commentary

Remove self-referential asides. The text should move, not announce its own
structure.

- "The rest of this essay explains…"
- "In this section, we'll…"
- "Let me walk you through…"
- "As we'll see…"
- "I want to explore…"

## Vague Declaratives

Sentences that announce importance without naming the specific thing. Cut or
replace with the specific thing.

- "The implications are significant"
- "The stakes are high"
- "The reasons are structural"
- "The consequences are real"
- "This is the deepest problem"

If a sentence calls something important/deep/structural without showing the
specific thing, restate the specific thing.

## Telling Instead of Showing

Announcing difficulty or significance rather than demonstrating it.

- "This is genuinely hard"
- "This is what X actually looks like"
- "This is what leadership actually looks like"
- "actually matters"

Show the concrete thing the claim rests on.
